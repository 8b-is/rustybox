Semantic Plausibility vs. Structural Correctness: When AI Visualization Outruns Quantum Mechanics

In Part 1, we explored how formal invariants and strict constraint bounds allow low-level systems—like static userlands and engine statevectors—to guarantee deterministic execution. Today, we turn to a fascinating pathology on the opposite end of the spectrum: how diffusion and generative visual models synthesize diagrams that are locally saturated with correct mathematical vocabulary, yet globally violate the underlying physics.

The three visual breakdowns generated below present a textbook case of what I term the Representational Constraint Hypothesis and its counterpart, Constraint-Closure Failures. Every local element (Bloch spheres, state vectors, Hadamard gates, phase angles, CNOTs) is visually and lexically authentic. But when joined together, the relational structure fails to commute.

1. The Bloch Sphere: Local Skeletal Precision with Annotation Drift

Of the three generated diagrams, the first is the closest to being genuinely educational because it contains a rigid mathematical skeleton that constrains the generative ornamentation:

$$\left|\psi\right\rangle = \alpha\left|0\right\rangle + \beta\left|1\right\rangle = \cos\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\left|0\right\rangle + e^{i\phi}\sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\left|1\right\rangle$$

Here, the parameterization of the single-qubit pure state on the Bloch sphere is mathematically exact, as is the normalization constraint \(|\alpha|^2 + |\beta|^2 = 1\). The poles \(\left|0\right\rangle\) and \(\left|1\right\rangle\) correctly frame the two-level Hilbert space, and the polar angle \(\theta\) and azimuthal angle \(\phi\) properly pinpoint the pure state vector \(|\psi\rangle\).

Where the diagram degrades is in its relational annotations:

  • Imprecise Semantic Assignment: The annotation claims that \(\theta\) "determines relative probability amplitudes". In reality, \(\theta\) controls the relative magnitudes of the two basis state amplitudes (\(\cos(\theta/2)\) vs \(\sin(\theta/2)\)), whereas the azimuthal angle \(\phi\) dictates the relative phase.
  • Geometric Duplication: The duplicated angle ticks (\(0^\circ, 30^\circ, 60^\circ\)) drawn along the equator fail to map coherently to the spherical coordinate geometry \(\theta \in [0, \pi]\) and \(\phi \in [0, 2\pi)\).

2. The Color Wheel Fallacy: Overgeneralizing Representation into Ontology

The second breakdown illustrates a deeper theoretical trap: it takes a valid visualization convention—mapping complex phase to hue—and quietly promotes that visual metaphor into physical ontology.

In complex analysis, a single amplitude \(\alpha = r e^{i\phi}\) can have its phase angle \(\phi \in [0, 2\pi)\) visualized around an HSL color wheel. However, the generated diagram assigns the four computational basis states of a two-qubit system—\(|00\rangle\), \(|01\rangle\), \(|10\rangle\), and \(|11\rangle\)—to intrinsic static colors (violet, cyan, green, and amber).

A general two-qubit pure state is represented by:

$$\left|\psi\right\rangle = c_{00}\left|00\right\rangle + c_{01}\left|01\right\rangle + c_{10}\left|10\right\rangle + c_{11}\left|11\right\rangle, \quad c_{ij} \in \mathbb{C}$$

For a normalized two-qubit pure state (\(d=4\)), eliminating an overall unphysical global phase leaves:

$$2(4) - 2 = 6 \text{ real degrees of freedom}$$

There is no two-dimensional color wheel or single phase ring that can parameterize the 6-dimensional manifold of a two-qubit state space (\(\mathbb{CP}^3\)).

3. Classical Signal-Flow vs. Quantum Unitary Operators

The third image is visually the most compelling, yet mathematically the most deceptive. It adopts the visual language of a technical block diagram—complete with Hadamard (\(H\)) gates, phase shifters (\(\theta\)), CNOT gates, and wave traces—while treating quantum state evolution as classical signals traveling down spatial wires.

  • Spatial Wires vs. Hilbert Space Evolution: Quantum interference occurs within the statevector itself as unitary operators recombine complex probability amplitudes across orthogonal basis states.
  • Unconnected Physical Parameters: The diagram introduces an explicit optical wavelength \(\lambda = 532\text{ nm}\), which has no role in an abstract gate-model quantum circuit.
  • Decoupled Operator Decomposition: The diagram renders a \(\mathrm{CNOT}\) gate as two separate boxes connected to independent propagating waves, rather than an entangling unitary operator \(\mathrm{CNOT}\left|a, b\right\rangle = \left|a, \, a \oplus b\right\rangle\).

Conclusion: Semantic Plausibility vs. Constraint Satisfaction

Whether building static, zero-dependency musl userlands like rustybox or designing quantum simulation statevectors, semantic plausibility is fundamentally weaker than constraint satisfaction. True reliability requires strict constraint closure: where every local relation commutes with the global laws of the system.
